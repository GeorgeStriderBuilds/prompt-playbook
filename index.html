Tier 1

Research

In plain words: ask one question well, and make sure the answer can be checked.

Goal: {{decision}}
Constraints: {{timeframe}}, sources to prefer: {{sources}}, exclude: {{exclude}}
Check: cite sources, separate verified facts from estimates, say what is still unverified.
Stop: if no reliable source exists, say so instead of filling the gap.
Output: {{outputFormat}} — success means: {{success}}

Worked example

Goal: decide whether to attend GitHub Universe virtually or in person. Constraints: Oct 28-29, 2026, official GitHub sources preferred, exclude paid-pass hype. Check: cite the official schedule and price, separate confirmed prices from estimates. Stop: do not register or pay without my yes. Output: a short comparison — success means I can choose virtual or in-person from it.

Tier 2

Shopping

In plain words: buy something once, with the limits and fit checked first.

Goal: {{item}} for {{useCase}}
Hard limits: budget {{budget}}, size/fit {{fit}}, must-haves: {{mustHaves}}, must-not-haves: {{mustNotHaves}}
Check: verify each limit against the listing or spec sheet, and name what the listing does NOT publish.
Stop: do not buy, and do not contact a seller, without a fresh yes naming the exact item.
Output: {{outputFormat}} — success means: {{success}}

Worked example

Goal: a motorcycle jacket for riding in the mid-to-high 60s. Hard limits: budget under $250, CE Level 1, Large torso with long arms, wind protection, must-not-haves: perforations. Check: verify sleeve length center-back-neck to wrist and back-pocket size against the 17.1 inch D3O Viper insert, and name what the listing does NOT publish. Stop: do not buy without a fresh yes naming the exact item. Output: a shortlist with dimensions checked — success means I can pick one without guessing on fit.

Tier 2

Build an app

In plain words: get an app built, checked live, and published without extras sneaking in.

Goal: {{appIdea}} for {{whoFor}}
Constraints: single file if possible, {{visibility}}, no signup, no upload if that matters
Check: show it working before publishing, test on phone size, check the live URL after upload, verify the live result (links, counts, versions) before calling it done.
Stop: no monetization, ads, extra features, or merging apps unless approved separately.
Output: {{outputFormat}} — success means: {{success}}

Worked example

Goal: Sound Lab, a 16-step beat maker for phone browsers. Constraints: single file, public, no signup, no upload. Check: show it working before publishing, test on phone size, check the live URL after upload, verify the live result before calling it done. Stop: no monetization, ads, or extra features unless approved separately. Output: a live Pages link — success means I can play a pattern on my phone.

Tier 3

Social post

In plain words: post once, approved by you first, without spamming anyone.

Goal: {{appOrIdea}} on {{platformFirst}}
Rules: Threads first; via connector, never browser commenting; "Link in bio." on Instagram, no em dashes, photo reuse cap of 2; comments only as reply-backs or on followed/community accounts, each uniquely written and individually approved; Instagram comments by George himself or none
Check: show the exact caption and image before posting, confirm the link is clickable where it should be.
Stop: max 1 feed post per platform per day, max 1-2 Threads comments per day hours apart, no bulk likes/follows/DMs, skip a platform entirely on any warning, no evasion methods.
Output: {{outputFormat}} — success means: {{success}}

Worked example

Goal: Glitch Studio mirror post on Threads first. Rules: via connector, never browser commenting, no em dashes, photo reuse cap of 2, caption decisive, not indecisive. Check: show the exact caption and image before posting, confirm the link is clickable. Stop: max 1 feed post that day, skip a platform entirely on any warning. Output: one approved caption and image — success means I can approve it in one message and it goes out exactly as approved.

Tier 4

Recurring job

In plain words: set a job once that checks something on a schedule and reports back.

Goal: {{jobTask}}
Schedule: {{schedule}} {{timezone}}
Rules: what it may do: {{mayDo}}; what it may never do: {{mayNeverDo}}; safety stop on warnings
Output: {{outputFormat}} — success means: {{success}}
Stop: implementation only after approval in chat, never from the job itself.

Worked example

Goal: check Glitch Studio and Ride Check for one genuinely useful improvement each. Schedule: daily 2:00pm America/New_York. Rules: what it may do: propose only; what it may never do: implement, upload, or change a repo; safety stop on any warning. Output: a proposal, report only — success means I can say yes or no to each item separately. Stop: implementation only after approval in chat, never from the job itself.

Prompt health check

Paste any prompt below. This is a simple word check, not AI judgment. It looks for the four parts your best prompts have: a goal, a constraint, a check step, and a stop rule. It cannot tell if a prompt is clever, only if those four parts are present.

Proof Ledger

A logbook, not a star rating. Each template shows where it really came from, what failure it prevents, and whether it has actually been reused in this app. Reuse counts start at 0 and are honest: the Oct 26 daily review found no template had been copied or reused in the app yet. Log a use below after you actually use a template, and the count and verdict update in this browser only. Nothing is uploaded.

Rules that travel

  • Draft before publish.
  • One decision per message.
  • Write the stop rule into the prompt.
  • No private follows. No em dashes in social copy. Photo reuse cap of 2.
  • Proposal first for app changes. Safety stops on any platform warning.
  • Social, revised Oct 26, 2026: no browser commenting; Threads comments only via connector, individually approved, max 1-2/day, reply-backs or followed/community accounts only; Instagram comments by George himself or none; max 1 feed post per platform per day.
  • Verify the live result (links, counts, versions) before calling work done.
  • No chaining: templates are used one at a time, not chained together, unless you ask for chaining separately.
